Research vision

Potential Future Research Directions

Four complementary directions extending my work on grounding-zone processes, ice-shelf cavity circulation, and ice–ocean interactions. These are proposed research directions, not completed projects.

01 · Ice dynamics

Tidal flexure, ice–ocean coupling, and parameterization

Investigate how tidal flexure, grounding-zone geometry, and ocean stratification control seawater exchange, basal melting, and cavity-scale overturning. Develop physically informed representations of these processes for larger-scale ocean and ice-sheet models.

Related ongoing research →
02 · Ocean circulation

Water-mass transformation across warm and cold ice-shelf cavities

Develop a common framework using water-mass transformation and residual overturning to understand ocean heat delivery, eddy and tidal transport, basal melting, and refreezing across contrasting Antarctic ice-shelf environments. Test which physical relationships are robust across warm and cold cavity regimes.

Related overturning research →
03 · Observations

Model-guided observations beneath ice shelves

Integrate satellite observations, in situ measurements, and process-resolving ocean simulations to identify mechanisms controlling ocean heat delivery and meltwater export. Use observing-system simulation experiments to guide the location, timing, and sampling strategies of autonomous under-ice platforms.

Related observation–model framework →
04 · Reduced theory

Connecting plume dynamics with cavity-scale circulation

Develop a reduced theoretical framework coupling buoyant meltwater-plume dynamics along the ice base with residual circulation in the cavity interior. Use water-mass transformation, isopycnal structure, and eddy-induced transport to connect ocean heat supply, plume evolution, and basal melting. Test the theory against high-resolution simulations and extend it to account for tidal ice motion and melt-driven changes in ice-shelf geometry.

This direction builds on the residual-plume theory and iterative plume–interior coupling presented at IGS 2026. Achieving a self-consistent steady solution and testing its predictive skill remain future objectives.

Unifying goal

From process understanding to prediction

Together, these directions seek to connect grounding-zone processes, cavity-scale ocean circulation, field observations, and simplified physical theory, ultimately improving representations of ice–ocean interactions in large-scale models.
